Keys for developers and their AI agents

Your agents run the code. Your phone holds the keys.

sealctl keeps API keys, SSH keys, encrypted volumes and backups locked until your phone says yes. When an agent needs a key, your phone asks, you tap Allow, and the key goes into that one command. The agent never sees it, and it never lands in a transcript or a .env file.

What sealctl does

Agents never see the key

The key goes into the environment of the one command that asked. Output is masked to ****, and reading a secret directly is refused inside an agent.

One tap, or none at your desk

The first approval of the day takes one tap. Back at the same desk, with your phone unlocked and on the same network, the session reopens by itself.

Locked when you leave

Keys live in memory for the session only. Leaving a trusted place, idle time or the 02:00 hard stop locks the machine and unmounts its volumes.

24 words on paper

Every key comes from 24 words you write by hand on a printed card. With them, sealctl recover opens anything sealctl sealed, with no phone or server.

Volumes and backups too

Encrypted APFS or LUKS volumes and restic backups open with the same words, mount with the session and lock with it.

Nothing goes missing silently

Your phone lists every key it hands out and every use. When you change the words, it shows what moved and what it couldn't find.

If we disappear, your keys don't.

Everything sealctl protects opens from your 24 words with the open-source command line tool. And sealctl unseal --all hands it all back under passwords of your own whenever you want to leave. No server, account or subscription stands between you and your data.
